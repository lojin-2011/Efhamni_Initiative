Our Story

Not a diagnosis. A different question.

EfhAMNI is a youth-led social initiative focused on dyslexia awareness, understanding, empowerment, and inclusion. EfhAMNI means "understand me" — and that's exactly where we start.

We believe children and young people with dyslexia should never be defined by their learning difficulties. They deserve an environment that understands how they learn, recognizes their strengths, and gives them real opportunities to discover their potential.

Our goal isn't only to help society understand dyslexia. It's to help every child understand themselves.

EfhAMNI is not a medical diagnosis or treatment provider. We work alongside qualified specialists, educators, schools, parents, and communities to build something bigger together.

Children & young people

Ages 6–18 with dyslexia — our primary focus, and the reason EfhAMNI exists.

Parents & caregivers

We help families understand dyslexia, recognise strengths, and find the right support.

Understanding Dyslexia

Dyslexia is a different way of processing language — not a measure of intelligence.

Dyslexia can affect reading, writing, and language processing. It does not mean someone is less intelligent or unable to learn. But children with dyslexia are often misunderstood — seen as lazy, unfocused, or uninterested, instead of understood for what they actually need.

THE OLD QUESTION
"What is wrong with this child?"
  • Focuses on deficits and difficulties
  • Labels children as lazy or careless
  • Measures worth by test scores alone
THE EFHAMNI QUESTION
"What are this child's strengths and potential?"
  • Focuses on strengths, creativity, and interests
  • Builds real skills and confidence
  • Opens doors instead of closing them

Creativity

Many children with dyslexia think visually and creatively — strengths that traditional classrooms rarely measure.

Potential

Given the right support, dyslexic learners build real, transferable skills across art, tech, and communication.

Opportunity

Awareness changes how parents, teachers, and communities respond — and that changes a child's whole path.

Our Vision

A society that sees strengths first.

A society that understands dyslexia, recognises the strengths of children and young people instead of focusing only on their difficulties, and gives every individual the chance to discover their potential, build real skills, and take an active part in their community.
